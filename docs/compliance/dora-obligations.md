**Framing.** The platform vendor is not a financial entity and has no direct obligations under DORA. Every obligation in this document reaches us **contractually and commercially**: DORA obliges our financial-entity (FE) customers, and Art. 28–30 force them to pass specific requirements into their contracts with us. "We must" in this document always means "an FE customer cannot lawfully buy or keep this service unless we provide this." Requirements are tagged [must] (hard legal obligation of the customer that we must satisfy to be purchasable) or [should] (best practice / competitive positioning / risk reduction).

## 1. Applicability Analysis

### 1.1 The platform as an ICT third-party service provider

DORA Art. 3(19) defines an ICT third-party service provider as "an undertaking providing ICT services"; Art. 3(21) defines ICT services as "digital and data services provided through ICT systems to one or more internal or external users on an ongoing basis". A subscription SaaS SOC — telemetry ingestion, detection, AI triage, console — is unambiguously an ICT service. Therefore: **whenever a customer is a financial entity under Art. 2(1), we are an ICT third-party service provider to that customer**, with no registration, act of designation, or size threshold on our side.

FE types realistically present in the SME self-serve target market (Art. 2(1)):

| FE type | Art. 2(1) point | SME-market realism |
|---|---|---|
| Payment institutions | (b) | High — many are 20–250 employees |
| E-money institutions | (f) | High |
| Investment firms | (e) | High — small/boutique firms |
| Crypto-asset service providers (MiCA-authorised) | (g) | High — mostly SME-sized |
| Insurance/reinsurance intermediaries | (n) | High volume, **but** Art. 2(3)(e) exempts intermediaries that are microenterprises or small/medium-sized enterprises — many will be out of DORA scope entirely |
| Management companies / small AIFMs | (i)/(k) | Medium; note Art. 2(3)(a) exempts sub-threshold AIFMs |

Consequence: with a self-serve funnel we **cannot know at signup whether a tenant is an FE**. DORA obligations arrive silently with the customer. Two structural responses are needed: (a) a DORA-ready contractual addendum offered proactively (Art. 30 requires the *customer* to obtain these clauses; if our clickthrough ToS cannot carry them, FEs must churn or negotiate — the latter breaks the self-serve model), and (b) FE self-identification at signup or in tenant settings so we know which tenants have Art. 28–30 needs.

Proportionality note: Art. 4(1)–(2) and Art. 16 apply a simplified regime to microenterprises and certain small FEs, and Art. 28(1) applies the third-party-risk rules "in accordance with the principle of proportionality". Small FEs will demand less paperwork than a bank — but Art. 30(2) minimum contract content and the Art. 28(3) register of information apply to **all** in-scope FEs regardless of size.

### 1.2 Does the service support "critical or important functions" of customers?

Art. 3(22) defines a critical or important function as one "the disruption of which would materially impair the financial performance of a financial entity, or the soundness or continuity of its services and activities, or the discontinued, defective or failed performance of that function would materially impair the continuing compliance of a financial entity with the conditions and obligations of its authorisation, or with its other obligations under applicable financial services law".

Assessment: **yes, in most deployments, and we should plan on the conservative answer.** The classification is made by each customer, not by us, but:

1. Chapter II of DORA itself obliges every FE to run ICT security capabilities: Art. 10(1) requires "mechanisms to promptly detect anomalous activities … including ICT network performance issues and ICT-related incidents, and to identify potential material single points of failure"; Art. 17 requires an ICT-related incident management process built on detection. An FE that outsources detection/monitoring to us is outsourcing part of its own regulatory compliance machinery — squarely within the "continuing compliance … with its other obligations under applicable financial services law" limb of Art. 3(22).
2. The failure mode is asymmetric: if our platform is down or blind, the FE loses its ability to detect and classify incidents (Art. 10, 17, 18) during the outage.
3. The ITS on the register of information (CIR 2024/2956, template layer linking ICT services to functions) forces the FE to record, per contract, whether the ICT service supports a critical or important function; security operations services are routinely recorded as such by competent-authority guidance and market practice.

Working assumption for all engineering and contractual planning: **we are an ICT third-party service provider of ICT services supporting critical or important functions**, which triggers the *full* Art. 30(3) clause set, the Art. 28(8) pre-contract due-diligence burden on customers, and the subcontracting RTS (EU) 2025/532. A customer may occasionally classify us as non-critical (e.g., they run a parallel in-house SOC); we should not price or design for that minority case.

### 1.3 CTPP designation risk (Art. 31 oversight framework)

Art. 31(2) criteria, as operationalised by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1502: systemic impact of a failure, systemic importance of the FEs relying on the provider, criticality of the functions supported, and substitutability of the provider. Designation is data-driven: the ESAs mine the registers of information reported annually by FEs (CIR 2024/2956 data), then designate under Art. 31(9). The first list (2025-11-18) contains 19 providers — hyperscalers, major data vendors, core ICT integrators.

Realistic assessment at SME-market scale: **low probability in the near term (2–4 years), not zero, and monotonically increasing with FE customer count.**

- The criticality-of-function criterion cuts against us being ignored (security monitoring is exactly the kind of function overseers care about), but the systemic-importance criterion cuts strongly in our favor: our target FEs are small payment/e-money/crypto firms, not G-SIBs. The first designation round shows the ESAs prioritising concentration among *systemically important* entities.
- However, every FE customer must list us in its register of information (§2.2), so our EU-financial-sector footprint is fully visible to the ESAs from year one and re-assessed annually.
- Art. 31(11) allows voluntary opt-in to oversight. **Recommendation: do not opt in.** Oversight (Art. 33–39) brings lead-overseer inspections, recommendations, remediation plans, and fees — costs disproportionate to any marketing benefit at our scale.
- Practical trigger to watch: if a material share of any member state's payment-institution or CASP population standardises on us, national competent authorities can flag concentration to the ESAs. Compliance agent to re-run this assessment annually or at 100 confirmed FE tenants, whichever comes first.
- Note also Art. 31(12)–(13): FEs may only use ICT third-party providers **established in a third country** that have been designated critical if that provider has established an EU subsidiary within 12 months of designation. Not currently relevant (assumes EU establishment), but constrains any future corporate-structure decision (OQ-7 data residency interacts with this).

### 2.2 Art. 28 — general principles and the register of information

- **Art. 28(3):** every FE must "maintain and update at entity level … a register of information in relation to all contractual arrangements on the use of ICT services provided by ICT third-party service providers", distinguishing services supporting critical or important functions, and report it at least yearly to its competent authority. CIR 2024/2956 fixes 15 linked templates covering: entity, ICT provider identification, contractual arrangement, ICT service, function supported, and the **subcontracting chain**.
- **Data we must supply to every FE customer** (they cannot complete the templates without us):
  - Provider identification: legal name, **LEI or EUID** (we should obtain an LEI — small cost, removes friction; CIR 2024/2956 provider-identification template expects a code), registered address, country, parent-undertaking identification.
  - ICT service type per the **closed Annex III taxonomy (S01–S19)** — we must determine and publish which code(s) our service maps to (security-services related code(s); exact mapping to be confirmed against Annex III text and stated once, consistently, to all customers).
  - Data-processing and storage locations (feeds the same answer as 30(2)(b)).
  - Whether the service supports a critical or important function — the FE decides, but expects our input.
  - **Subcontracting chain with rank**: the templates model multi-tier chains. We must provide our material subcontractors in a form customers can transcribe: cloud provider (region), **LLM provider for AI triage**, email vendor, etc., each with identification data.
  - Substitutability/exit-relevant fields (alternative providers, reintegration feasibility) — the FE assesses, we provide facts.
- **Art. 28(8):** before contracting, FEs must run due diligence ("assess whether the ICT third-party service provider covers relevant information security standards" — for critical/important functions, "the most up-to-date and highest quality information security standards"). Expect security questionnaires at self-serve scale → a public trust page + standardised due-diligence pack is the scalable answer.
- **MVP coverage:** none of this exists as product/collateral. The underlying facts are known (architecture is documented; subcontractors are enumerable). **Gap:** a maintained, versioned "register-of-information data pack" (COMP-2, and product opportunity §4.1).

### 2.4 Art. 17–23 — incident management and reporting: our support duties

The obligations sit on the FE, but they are impossible to discharge without data from us. Two distinct scenarios:

**Scenario A — incident in the customer's environment, detected by our platform.** The FE must: detect (Art. 10), record and classify (Art. 17(2), Art. 18(1) with the RTS 2024/1772 criteria — clients/counterparts affected, duration and service downtime, geographical spread, data losses, criticality of services affected, economic impact), and for **major** incidents report to its competent authority (Art. 19(1)) on the RTS 2025/301 / ITS 2025/302 clock: **initial notification within 4 hours of classification as major and no later than 24 hours after detection; intermediate report within 72 hours; final report within 1 month.**
- MVP coverage is genuinely strong here — this is the product: detection ≤60s e2e (AC-37), alert detail with linked raw events, timestamps, affected assets, action history (AC-74), correlation groups (AC-43), occurrence counts and first/last-seen (AC-41/42), UTC audit trail (AC-83). AI triage summaries (AC-48) give the plain-language description the report templates ask for.
- Gap: nothing maps alert data onto the **RTS 2024/1772 classification criteria** or the ITS 2025/302 report fields, and console-only alerting (out-of-scope #11, OQ-6) means a 4-hour classification clock can expire before anyone looks at the console. OQ-6 is therefore not just a UX question — for FE customers, notification latency is a regulatory-deadline feed. See COMP-6 and product opportunities §4.

**Scenario B — incident in OUR platform affecting the customer.** An outage or breach of our platform can itself be a reportable major ICT incident **of the FE** (their detection capability is impaired; under Art. 18/RTS 2024/1772 the criticality-of-services criterion is engaged). Art. 30(2)(f) obliges us to assist; the FE's 4h/24h/72h clocks run against information only we hold.
- MVP coverage: internal observability only (AC-91, AC-5). **No mechanism informs tenants of platform incidents. This is the highest-severity gap in the entire map.** See COMP-5.
- Additionally, Art. 19(1) third subparagraph: FEs "may outsource … the reporting obligations under this Article to a third-party service provider", subject to competent-authority conditions — an explicit hook for a premium feature (§4.2), not an MVP requirement.

### 2.5 RTS (EU) 2025/532 — subcontracting conditions (applies since 2025-07-22)

When the ICT service supports critical or important functions, the FE must, per the RTS: perform due diligence over the **whole subcontracting chain**, ensure the contract specifies which services may be subcontracted and under what conditions (echoing Art. 30(2)(a)), retain the right to be notified of and object to material subcontracting changes, and be able to monitor the chain.

Consequence for us — the chain we must disclose and keep current, minimum viable list from the MVP design:
1. Cloud/IaaS provider hosting the stamp (compute, PG, ES, Redis if managed) — region-specific.
2. **LLM/model provider for AI triage** (worker-triager, design §2/§4.2) — including its own hosting location and any further sub-processing. Model routing by tier (business model consequence #5) means potentially *multiple* model providers; each is a subcontractor. A model-provider switch is a notifiable subcontracting change for every FE customer.
3. Transactional email provider (verification emails AC-1, future notifications).
4. Any other processors touching tenant data (CDN/WAF in front of console or ingest-gateway, error-tracking/APM SaaS if tenant data reaches it).
MVP coverage: none (no register, no notification process). See COMP-2, COMP-3.

## 3. MVP Design-Time Requirements (numbered findings)

Each finding is a testable requirement for the `platform-foundation-mvp` feature or its immediate backlog. **[must]** = hard prerequisite before signing (or knowingly retaining) any financial-entity customer; **[should]** = best practice / pre-GA. "MVP-blocking" = should be built or provided-for in the current MVP cycle because retrofit is expensive; "pre-GA backlog" = required before FE go-to-market but not in this engineering cycle.

**COMP-1 [must] — Resolve and pin data residency (Art. 30(2)(b); CIR 2024/2956 location fields).** MVP-blocking (it is PRD OQ-7 and gates signup messaging). Requirement: the launch stamp's region(s) and country(ies) for processing and storage are decided, documented per component (PG, ES, Redis, object storage when added, backups), and exposed in customer-facing terms; an internal control exists such that any change of processing/storage location triggers customer notification before the change. Test: a documented location matrix exists; a simulated region change produces a notification obligation in the process runbook.

**COMP-2 [must] — Maintain a versioned subcontractor register including the LLM provider (Art. 30(2)(a); RTS (EU) 2025/532; CIR 2024/2956 subcontracting-chain templates).** MVP-blocking as a document; automation later. Requirement: a machine-readable register (name, identifier/LEI where available, service provided, data touched, processing locations, rank in chain) covering at minimum cloud provider, every LLM/model provider used by worker-triager, and email vendor; changes are versioned and dated; FE customers are notified of material changes with a contractual objection window. The model-routing design (fast model vs. deep investigation) must treat "add/switch model provider" as a controlled, notifiable change, not a config tweak. Test: register exists, enumerates the actual MVP model provider, and a provider swap produces a new register version + notification artifact.

**COMP-3 [must] — LLM provider data-handling terms compatible with Art. 30(2)(b)–(c) (and PRD flag S-6).** MVP-blocking (model choice is being made now). Requirement: the contracted model-provider terms guarantee (a) no training on tenant data, (b) defined processing locations disclosed in COMP-1/COMP-2, (c) defined retention (ideally zero) of prompts/completions, (d) confidentiality terms we can pass through to FE customers. Prompt content (AC-48/52 triage context) is tenant security-event data — the most sensitive data class we hold. Test: executed provider terms on file matching these four properties; triage prompt-assembly design references the approved provider list.

**COMP-4 [must] — Tenant data export and offboarding for paid tenants (Art. 30(2)(d), 30(3)(f)).** Pre-GA backlog; MVP must not preclude it. Requirement: (a) self-service or support-assisted bulk export of events (versioned schema per AC-31), alerts including triage output and state history, asset inventory including merge audit (AC-24), and the tenant audit log (AC-84), in documented machine-readable formats; (b) a documented offboarding sequence for paid tenants — defined transition period, export window, then verified deletion reusing the trial-purge machinery (AC-10) with the same audit-verifiable outcome; (c) committed transition-support terms in the DORA addendum. Test: QA exports a seeded tenant and re-validates the export against the published schema; post-offboarding queries return empty as in AC-10.

**COMP-5 [must] — Customer-facing platform-incident notification (Art. 30(2)(f), 30(3)(b); feeds customers' Art. 19 deadlines).** Pre-GA backlog; the *design hook* is MVP-blocking. Requirement: when a platform incident materially affects a tenant's service (ingest outage, detection pipeline stall, data breach, missed SLOs), affected tenants are notified through an out-of-console channel (email/status page/webhook) within a committed time (target: ≤ 4 hours for incidents likely to be classification-relevant to FEs, aligned to the RTS 2025/301 initial-notification window their own clock depends on), with per-tenant impact statements (which stages, what time window, whether data was lost — AC-91's per-tenant/per-stage labels make this computable). MVP hook: AC-91 metrics and internal ops alerts (AC-5, AC-40) must carry tenant-impact attribution so a future notifier can consume them. Test: fault injection on a pipeline stage yields a per-tenant impact record within the committed window.

**COMP-6 [must] — Incident evidence completeness and UTC timeline integrity (supports customers' Art. 17(2), 18(1), 20 template fields).** Largely already in MVP — verify, don't build. Requirement: every alert exposes machine-readable: detection time, first/last event time, affected assets/users, rule + version, correlation group, full state-change history with actors and UTC timestamps. This is AC-35, AC-41–43, AC-45, AC-74, AC-83. Additional testable delta: these fields are retrievable via API for a closed alert for at least the tenant's retention period, and the audit trail for an alert survives event-data retention expiry (audit retention ≥365 days per AC-84 already guarantees this). Test: contract test asserting the field set on `GET /v1/alerts/{id}` for a closed, aged alert.

**COMP-7 [must] — FE customer identification and DORA contractual addendum (Art. 30(1): rights and obligations "clearly allocated and set out in writing"; Art. 30(2)/(3) content).** Pre-GA backlog (legal work, not engineering) plus one small MVP-blocking product hook: tenant signup/settings capture "regulated financial entity (EU)?" so FE tenants are identifiable, and the addendum flow can be offered self-serve. Requirement: a standard DORA addendum covering every 30(2)(a)–(i) and 30(3)(a)–(f) item, offered without bespoke negotiation at Core/Pro tiers. Art. 30(4): consider alignment with public standard contractual clauses when available. Test: legal sign-off checklist mapping each 30(2)/30(3) point to an addendum clause; tenant record carries the FE flag.

**COMP-8 [should] — Committed, customer-visible service levels (Art. 30(2)(e), 30(3)(a)).** Pre-GA backlog. Requirement: publish committed targets (availability of console/ingest/detection pipeline; detection latency; support response) derived from the internal SLOs (AC-3/30/33/37/48), and expose per-tenant attainment (the per-tenant metrics of AC-89/AC-51 already exist; this is a reporting surface, not new instrumentation). Test: a tenant admin can view/download monthly service-level attainment.

**COMP-9 [should] — Third-party assurance to satisfy audit/access rights at multi-tenant scale (Art. 30(3)(e); Art. 28(8) due diligence).** Pre-GA backlog; start now because certification lead time is 6–12 months. Requirement: ISO/IEC 27001 certification and/or SOC 2 Type II covering the stamp, plus a due-diligence pack (architecture, isolation design AC-79–82, pen-test summaries); contractual audit clause offering certification-based assurance, pooled audits, and for-cause escalation; dedicated-stamp tier (ADR-0001) positioned as the answer for customers whose regulators insist on unrestricted audit. Test: certification scope statement covers all components in design §2.

**COMP-10 [should] — Competent-authority cooperation process (Art. 30(2)(g)).** Pre-GA backlog, organisational. Requirement: named regulatory contact, documented intake/response procedure with legal review, and a technical path to produce single-tenant disclosures without exposing other tenants (tenant-scoped queries per AC-79–81 make this feasible; the procedure must forbid ad-hoc cross-tenant pulls). Test: tabletop exercise producing a single-tenant evidence package.

**COMP-11 [should] — Documented and tested business continuity / DR for the stamp (Art. 30(3)(c)).** Pre-GA backlog. Requirement: BCP/DR document with RTO/RPO per component, backup/restore procedures for PG/ES/Redis, and at least an annual restore test; the AC-90 clean-environment deploy + smoke test is the seed of the restore drill. Test: restore drill report with measured RTO/RPO.

## 4. Product Opportunities (customer-side DORA obligations → features)

Handoffs to **product-manager** — flagged only, not specified here. Each converts an FE customer's own DORA duty into willingness-to-pay.

1. **"DORA evidence pack" / register-of-information export.** One-click export of everything the customer needs from us for their CIR 2024/2956 register templates (our identification, service type code, locations, subcontracting chain) — ideally in a format aligned to the templates' structure. Extends naturally to evidence for their Art. 28(8) due-diligence file. (Builds on COMP-2/COMP-13.)
2. **Incident classification and reporting assistant.** Map alert/correlation data onto the RTS 2024/1772 classification criteria (duration, services affected, data losses, geographical spread), track the customer's 4h/24h/72h/1-month clocks (RTS 2025/301), and pre-fill ITS 2025/302 report fields from alert evidence (AC-74's field set is most of a report annex). Long-range: Art. 19(1) explicitly permits FEs to outsource the reporting itself to a third party — a premium managed-reporting tier. Strong fit with the AI-triage differentiator.
3. **Service-level attainment and resilience reporting.** Per-tenant monthly reports (uptime, detection latency, our incident history) that the customer can file as evidence of their Art. 28(4)/(6)-driven ongoing monitoring of us. Cheap: AC-89/AC-51 metrics already exist per tenant.
4. **Retention tiers aligned to FE evidence needs.** FEs need incident records and monitoring evidence beyond SME-typical 14–90-day hot retention (plan-config table, PRD §4); a compliance retention add-on (e.g., 1yr+ alert/audit retention, already the Dedicated default) is a natural upsell — and the audit log's 365-day floor (AC-84) is a marketable baseline.
5. **Exit-strategy support as a trust feature.** Documented export formats and a tested offboarding path (COMP-4) marketed as "no lock-in / DORA exit-ready" — FEs must document exit strategies for critical ICT providers (Art. 28(8)) and will favor vendors who make that paperwork easy.
6. **FE onboarding lane.** The FE flag (COMP-7) can trigger a guided flow: DORA addendum e-sign, evidence pack download, notification-channel setup — keeping FE acquisition self-serve instead of sales-led.
